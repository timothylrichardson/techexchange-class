After the course

Keep going

The eight hours were a start. These are the tools, shortcuts, and free courses that will take you further — all of them free, all of them things you can open today.

Section 1AI assistants worth knowing

In Class 1 you met Perplexity. These are the others. All are free to start, and all of them work in the browser you already have — nothing to install. Ask them to explain something confusing, draft a letter, or summarise something long.

Claude

claude.ai

Built by Anthropic. Strong at reading long documents, writing, and thinking through a problem step by step.

Best forPasting in a long letter, lease, or benefits notice and asking what it actually says.

Gemini

gemini.google.com

Google's assistant. Works inside Gmail, Docs, Drive, and Sheets — the tools from Class 2.

Best forHelp writing a document or email without leaving the app you're already in.

ChatGPT

chatgpt.com

The most widely used. Handles text, pictures, voice, and files in one conversation.

Best forTalking to it out loud instead of typing, if typing is slow going.

Perplexity

perplexity.ai

Answers questions and lists its sources underneath, so you can check where the answer came from. No account needed.

Best forAnything you need to verify — the sources are right there to click.

Microsoft Copilot

copilot.microsoft.com

Built into Windows and into Word, Excel, and Outlook.

Best forHelp inside Word or Excel, on the laptop you took home.

Grok

grok.com

Made by xAI, connected to X (formerly Twitter). Geared toward current events and live discussion.

Best forFollowing breaking news and what people are saying about it right now.
Remember from Class 1

AI is confident even when it is wrong. Use it to understand something. Then check anything that matters — names, dates, prices, phone numbers, medical or legal advice — against the real .gov or .org website.

Never paste your passphrase, your Social Security number, or your bank details into any of these.

Two worth learning next

⊞ + L locks your screen instantly. Use it any time you walk away from your laptop in a library, a café, or a clinic waiting room.

Ctrl + Shift + T brings back the browser tab you closed by mistake. It works several times in a row.

Section 3Free places to keep learning

All free. No payment details, no trial that turns into a bill.

Courses and tutorials

  • GCFGlobal.orgShort written tutorials on computer basics, Microsoft Office, Google apps, and internet safety. Closest match to what we covered in class.
  • DigitalLearn.orgBeginner courses from the Public Library Association. Go at your own pace, repeat anything.
  • Applied Digital Skills (Google)Free video lessons on Google Workspace, online safety, and job searching.

Typing practice

  • TypingClub.comStructured lessons from the very beginning. Fifteen minutes a day makes a real difference within a month.
  • Keybr.comAdjusts to the letters you keep getting wrong.

Staying safe and managing passwords

  • BitwardenA free password manager. It remembers a different strong passphrase for every account so you don't have to. This is the one we demonstrated in Class 2 — setting it up takes about ten minutes at home.
  • Have I Been PwnedType in your email address to see whether it has turned up in a company data breach. If it has, change that password.
  • StaySafeOnline.orgPlain-language guidance on passwords, scams, and protecting your personal information.
